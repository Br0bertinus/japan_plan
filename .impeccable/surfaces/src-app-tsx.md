## Scope and mode

`src/App.tsx` is the single-page trip journey prototype. Visitor mode: Experience.

## Audience and job

Kate, Rob, Jules, and Milo need to make four choices that unlock booking and deeper research: Travel dates, Tokyo hotel, Middle-of-trip route, and Final city. They also need to understand the route evidence behind each choice.

## Content and action

A compact four-step semantic anchor agenda sits within the inky-blue fish-print opening and acts as the group-call discussion guide. Its Travel dates row anchors to a May / June 2027 shared Japan hotel-range picker: check-in is arrival in Japan, check-out is departure from Japan, and check-out is not counted as a hotel night. Tokyo hotel asks for a stay experience first, then a hotel: a central/iconic splurge through Park Hyatt Tokyo in West Shinjuku, HOTEL GROOVE SHINJUKU in Kabukicho, or Hotel Toranomon Hills in Toranomon/Minato; or a lower-key retreat through TRUNK(HOTEL) YOYOGI PARK in Tomigaya near central Shibuya or YUEN BETTEI DAITA in more residential Daita near Shimokitazawa. Middle-of-trip route is an ordered driving-route builder: Lake Nojiri, James Brown, Shirahone, and Okuhida are optional stops; Chubu-Sangaku remains a desired experience; Kyoto or Tokyo supplies the endpoint. Final city resolves Kyoto versus a Tokyo return. The agenda and persistent rail use the same four decision names and order.

## Chosen direction

The opening uses a centered two-zone composition above 1200px on a solid `#173B57` inky-blue field. A left-aligned content column contains `JAPAN`, `October 4 Team Meeting`, the Jules/Milo/Kate/Rob traveler order, and the four-row agenda; a separate right illustration column rotates the Fish, Cherry, Temple, and Fuji paper prints without overlap. The visible selector and current-print indicator are absent. One discreet overlaid 44px pause/play target controls the sequence, while reduced motion keeps Fish static and hides the control. Cherry and Temple use larger per-print scale and positioning inside the stable clipped frame. Tablet and mobile stack title/travelers, a deliberately cropped active print, then agenda with left-aligned reading text. The date planner uses a guided `IN` then `OUT` sequence and one direct range output. The first three decision mastheads share a narrow left destination stage beneath the numbered marker: airplane for dates, paired lodging beds for Tokyo, and an outdoor onsen for the middle route. One logical traveler group departs the header, advances once through those stages, and finishes in a compact persistent dance below the summary. The route builder owns the Kyoto/Tokyo endpoint, and the final sheet is a read-only summary of the existing stores. A folded-note launcher creates draggable, keyboard-movable, section-anchored notes that remain in the browser only.

## Constraints

The agenda remains navigation only. Date entry supports click, tap, and keyboard; never silently reorders values; and counts hotel nights without check-out. Tokyo uses official ward/rail geometry and five operator-source hotel points; its recovered `100%` view is the full `0 0 1000 720` extent. Hotel, ward, and station labels use distinct semantic tiers plus deterministic collision suppression, with active hotel annotations winning. Subjective pros and tradeoffs remain labeled `Planning take`, while sourced context stays in a disclosure. Tokyo and Central Japan share responsive map-section gutters and a wide-screen maximum; Central Japan gives the map the flexible track and bounds the route controls column. The Central Japan map includes complete Niigata, a 100-mile scale, one diamond marker language, no fixed route, and a minimal key. A single travel-order component keeps selected rows first and unselected rows below `Not in route`; totals derive from displayed cached OSRM legs and use centralized miles formatting. The three SVG props share a `720 × 360` view box and typed approach, entry, four-occupant, occlusion, and exit anchors. A page-load-only finite-state journey advances header → dates → hotels → middle → finale. Maximum downward scroll is the monotonic progress source: positions interpolate continuously, upward/revisited distance does not reverse or replay, and limbs stop shortly after new progress pauses. A body-level transit overlay uses actual measured stage coordinates, remains above content but below sticky notes/navigation, never intercepts input, and renders 88px desktop or 64px compact articulated figures. Public trajectory guides remain hidden. Prop-local handoffs preserve airplane clipping and body occlusion, while bed and onsen heads render in final unmasked layers above all decorative strokes; bed pairing is Jules + Kate and Milo + Rob, and onsen depth is Jules/Kate foreground heads with Milo/Rob rear busts. Four onsen bundles persist without clones. The typed ten-second finale includes hands-up, full turns, position swaps, and regrouping and persists until `Replay journey` or reload. Replay resets only animation, focuses `#departure`, and preserves all planning and note data. On collapsed layouts, stages sit after heading copy and before controls, never over maps or text. The route builder is the sole final-city owner. Summary rows update through the existing browser events and contain no duplicate forms. Notes are versioned under `japan-trip-sticky-notes-v1`, never synchronized, never included in URLs or summary data, and clamp to reachable bounds. All notes render in one body-level overlay above section stacking contexts; each note retains clickable controls and an always-visible Delete action while the overlay itself does not block the page. Reduced motion skips travel and looping motion while preserving static settled state.
